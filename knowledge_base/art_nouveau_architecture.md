# Art Nouveau Architecture

## Overview
Art Nouveau emerged in the 1890s and flourished until around 1910, as a reaction against 
historical revivalism. It sought to unify architecture with the decorative arts through 
flowing, organic forms inspired by nature.

## Defining Features
- Sinuous, curving lines inspired by plant stems, flowers, and organic forms
- Asymmetrical facades and irregular window shapes
- Extensive use of wrought iron in balconies, railings, and structural ornament
- Integration of stained glass, mosaic, and sculptural detail into the building's structure
- Whiplash curves and "coup de fouet" motifs in decorative ironwork and carving

## Conservation Considerations
- Wrought iron detailing is prone to rust, which can cause surrounding stone or 
  masonry to crack as corrosion expands
- Curved and irregular glasswork is difficult and costly to replace like-for-like
- Decorative plasterwork with fine organic detail is fragile and easily damaged by 
  inappropriate cleaning or humidity swings
- Many Art Nouveau buildings are relatively young (under 150 years), so original 
  fixings and finishes are only now reaching a typical failure age
