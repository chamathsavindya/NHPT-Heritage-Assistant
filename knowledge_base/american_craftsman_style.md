# American Craftsman Style

## Overview
The Craftsman style grew out of the American Arts and Crafts movement in the early 20th 
century (c. 1905–1930), emphasizing handcrafted quality and natural materials as a reaction 
against industrialized, mass-produced Victorian ornament.

## Defining Features
- Low-pitched gabled roofs with wide, overhanging eaves
- Exposed roof rafters and decorative knee braces visible under the eaves
- Full or partial-width front porches with thick, tapered columns often resting on 
  stone or brick piers
- Extensive use of natural materials: wood shingles, stone, and brick
- Built-in wood cabinetry, window seats, and handcrafted interior woodwork
- Multi-pane upper window sashes over single-pane lower sashes

## Conservation Considerations
- Exposed rafter tails and eave brackets are highly exposed to weather and prone to rot
- Original wood shingle siding requires more frequent maintenance than brick or stucco alternatives
- Stone/brick porch piers can settle unevenly over time, affecting the porch structure above
- Because this style overlaps visually with American Foursquare (low rooflines, front 
  porches, early 20th-century American residential design), the two are sometimes 
  difficult to distinguish without checking specific details like roof pitch, eave 
  depth, and porch column proportions
