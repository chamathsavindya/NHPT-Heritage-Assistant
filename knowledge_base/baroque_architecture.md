# Baroque Architecture

## Overview
Baroque architecture developed in late 16th-century Italy and spread across Catholic and 
later Protestant Europe through the 18th century. Where Renaissance design prized calm 
proportion, Baroque architects pursued drama, movement, and theatrical contrast between 
light and shadow.

## Defining Features
- Dynamic curved forms: convex/concave facades, oval floor plans, undulating cornices
- Grand scale and axial planning, with dominant central domes
- Rich ornamentation: gilded stucco, dramatic sculpture, trompe l'oeil ceiling painting
- Dramatic, staged use of light through hidden or concentrated light sources
- Integration of architecture, sculpture, and painting into unified decorative schemes

## Conservation Considerations
- Ornamental stucco is fragile and sensitive to humidity fluctuations; delamination 
  from the substrate is a common failure
- Large unreinforced masonry domes are prone to meridional cracking as they settle
- Historic gilding is easily damaged by inappropriate cleaning agents
- Elaborate carved stonework with deep undercuts traps moisture, encouraging salt 
  crystallization that fractures fine detail from within
