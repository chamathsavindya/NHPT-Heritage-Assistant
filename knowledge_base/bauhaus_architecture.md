# Bauhaus Architecture

## Overview
Bauhaus architecture emerged from the German Bauhaus school (1919–1933), rejecting 
ornament in favor of functionalist design principles. It became hugely influential on 
20th-century Modernist architecture worldwide.

## Defining Features
- Strict functionalism: "form follows function," with minimal to no applied decoration
- Flat roofs and clean, unadorned geometric volumes
- Extensive use of glass curtain walls and steel-frame construction
- Open floor plans enabled by structural steel/concrete framing rather than load-bearing walls
- Industrial materials (glass, steel, concrete) left honestly exposed rather than disguised

## Conservation Considerations
- Flat roofs, common in this style, are more prone to water pooling and leaks than 
  pitched roofs if drainage isn't well maintained
- Early reinforced concrete (common in period Bauhaus buildings) can suffer from 
  "concrete cancer" — steel reinforcement corroding and expanding, cracking the concrete
- Large glass curtain walls have significant thermal and structural sealant maintenance needs
- Because ornament was intentionally minimized, damage is often more visually obvious 
  and disruptive to the design intent than on more ornate historic styles
