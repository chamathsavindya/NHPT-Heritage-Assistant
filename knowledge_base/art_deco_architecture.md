# Art Deco Architecture

## Overview
Art Deco flourished internationally from the 1920s to the 1940s, taking its name from the 
1925 Paris Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs. It reflects the era's fascination 
with speed, industry, and modernity, blending geometric abstraction with luxurious materials.

## Defining Features
- Geometric ornament: zigzags, chevrons, sunbursts, and stepped "ziggurat" forms
- Vertical massing with stepped setbacks, especially in American skyscrapers
- Modern materials: chrome, stainless steel, opaque colored glass (Vitrolite), reinforced concrete
- Stylized motifs drawn from Egyptian, Aztec, and machine-age imagery
- Later "Streamline Moderne" variants softened Deco's angularity into aerodynamic curves

## Conservation Considerations
- Decorative chrome and bronze detailing corrodes once protective coatings fail
- Terrazzo and cast stone can crack along aggregate boundaries; repairs must match 
  original materials to avoid visible patching
- Pigmented structural glass (Vitrolite) panels are often irreplaceable if cracked
- Cast relief panels on now-degraded mechanical fixings pose a falling-hazard risk and 
  should be checked for movement, not just visible cracking
