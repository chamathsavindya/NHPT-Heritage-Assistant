# Ancient Egyptian Architecture

## Overview
Ancient Egyptian architecture spans over 3,000 years (c. 3100–30 BCE), producing some of 
the most enduring monumental structures in human history, including pyramids, temples, 
and tomb complexes, built primarily to serve religious and funerary purposes.

## Defining Features
- Massive stone construction using post-and-lintel systems (no true arches)
- Monumental scale, exemplified by pyramids and colossal temple pylons
- Heavy use of hieroglyphic relief carving covering walls, columns, and ceilings
- Columns often shaped to resemble papyrus, lotus, or palm forms
- Symmetrical, axial layouts aligned with astronomical or religious significance
- Battered (inward-sloping) walls for structural stability

## Conservation Considerations
- Exposed relief carvings are vulnerable to sand erosion and salt crystallization, 
  especially near groundwater-affected sites
- Painted surfaces (where preserved) are extremely light- and humidity-sensitive
- Structural cracking in large stone lintels is a serious safety concern given the 
  weight involved
- Modern tourism and rising groundwater from irrigation are significant, ongoing threats 
  distinct from natural weathering alone
