# American Foursquare Architecture

## Overview
The American Foursquare emerged around 1890 and remained popular through the 1930s as a 
practical, affordable response to the ornate excess of Victorian styles. It was widely 
built across the United States, often from mail-order house plan catalogs.

## Defining Features
- Simple, boxy, roughly square floor plan (hence "Foursquare")
- Two-and-a-half stories with a large central dormer window on the roof
- Low-pitched, hipped (four-sided sloping) roof
- Full-width front porch supported by simple square or round columns
- Symmetrical facade with a central front door and evenly spaced windows
- Minimal exterior ornamentation compared to earlier Victorian styles

## Conservation Considerations
- Front porches are a common failure point: wood posts and flooring are exposed to 
  weather and prone to rot at ground contact points
- Low-pitched hipped roofs can be prone to drainage issues if gutters aren't well maintained
- Original wood siding/cladding requires regular repainting to prevent moisture ingress
- Because many were mass-produced from standard plans, replacement parts (millwork, 
  trim profiles) are sometimes easier to source or replicate than on more unique, 
  architect-designed buildings
